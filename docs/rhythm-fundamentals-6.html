Rhythm Fundamentals: Dividing the Beat Again

Part 6 · Triplets and other tuplets · Feeling subdivisions in 4/4

We've explored the different feelings of 4/4, 3/4, 5/4 and 7/4. Now let's return to the familiar four-beat bar. This time, we won't change the number of beats. We'll change what happens inside each beat.

One beat, many possibilities

In 4/4, we have four quarter-note beats in each bar. So far, we've divided each beat into one quarter note, two eighth notes, or four sixteenth notes. But there is no reason we have to stop there.

What if we divide one beat into three equal parts? We get a triplet. We can also fit five equal notes into one beat (a quintuplet), six (a sextuplet), or even seven (a septuplet).

Keep one steady foot tap

Each box below is one beat, no matter how many notes are inside it. Tap your foot once per box. Say “ta” evenly for every dot.

In standard notation, a small number (such as 3, 5 or 7) is placed over or under the grouped notes to show the tuplet. Here we'll use simple dots and the number above each group, so you can focus on the sound.

Try it gently

Start with one, two and three notes per beat. Keep tapping four steady beats with your foot, and say “ta” on each note. A triplet may feel unfamiliar at first. That's the point of the exercise: to let it become familiar.

When you're ready, add four, five, six or seven notes per beat. The foot tap stays exactly the same speed; only the notes between the taps change. Slow the tempo down for the faster subdivisions.

Endless rhythm practice in 4/4

Each bar has four beats. The player chooses how many equal notes fit inside each beat, using only the subdivisions you select. Tap your foot steadily, and say “ta” for every note you hear.

Listen to the difference

Two, three, four or seven notes may all fit into the same amount of time. What changes is the spacing between them. Over time, you'll start to recognise these subdivisions by their feel, without needing to count every note.
