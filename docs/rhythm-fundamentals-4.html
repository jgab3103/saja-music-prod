Rhythm Fundamentals: Feeling Three Beats

Part 4 · Introducing 3/4 time · Years 7 and above

In Part 3, you practised keeping a steady pulse with your foot while saying “ta” each time a new note began. You didn't need to say all the counting syllables aloud. Let's keep doing exactly that — but change the way the beats are grouped.

From four beats to three

Until now, our bars have contained four beats. We call this 4/4 time. The top number tells us there are four beats in each bar; the bottom number, 4, tells us that a quarter note (crotchet) is worth one beat.

Now we'll try 3/4 time. The quarter note is still one beat, but there are only three beats in each bar. The pulse hasn't changed speed — it simply comes around in groups of three rather than four.

Hear the difference in the feel

4/4: ONE · two · three · four | ONE · two · three · four

3/4: ONE · two · three | ONE · two · three

Try tapping a gentle, even pulse with your foot. In 3/4, notice how the first beat of each group comes around sooner. That repeating ONE-two-three feeling is familiar from a waltz.

You can think “ONE-two-three” at first to find the feel. Once it's comfortable, let the counting go and keep tapping.

Keep feeling the pulse

Just as before, tap your foot on every beat and say “ta” only when a new note begins. Keep the pulse going through longer notes, and don't speed up when you see eighth or sixteenth notes.

We are not learning new note values today. We are learning to feel three beats as one repeating group. That is the difference between 4/4 and 3/4.

Endless rhythm practice in 3/4

Choose your note values and start slowly. The player creates new three-beat bars continuously. As one bar plays, you can look ahead to the next. Keep tapping your foot, and say “ta” on each new note.

Choose your note values

Start with quarter and eighth notes. A dotted half note fills an entire 3/4 bar. Whole notes aren't included because they last four beats — longer than one 3/4 bar.

What does 3/4 feel like?

After a few minutes, try listening for the beginning of each three-beat group without counting. You may notice a gentle turning or swaying quality, like a waltz. Keep the pulse even and let the rhythm move over it.
