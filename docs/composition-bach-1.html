Composition Study: Bach Chorales · Part 1

The Bach Chorale Explorer

Listening, reading and investigating four-part writing

A chorale is a hymn melody associated with the Lutheran tradition. Johann Sebastian Bach harmonised many such melodies for four voices. Each voice has its own melodic shape, but together they create an evolving harmonic texture.

In this study, we will learn to compose by investigating how music is made. Rather than starting with a list of rules, we will listen to Bach, take his music apart and discover how the individual parts work together.

1. Explore four-part writing

Four-part writing is often described as SATB: soprano, alto, tenor and bass. The soprano often carries the hymn melody, the bass shapes the harmonic foundation, and the alto and tenor connect the outer voices. Listen to any combination of voices, then follow the score one sonority at a time.

2. Composition exercises

Choose two or three measures of a chorale. Use manuscript paper or a piano to explore alternative versions of Bach’s music and different ways of representing four-part harmony.

  • Reduce all four voices from four individual staves onto a grand staff, as though arranging the chorale for piano.
  • Take a chorale presented on a grand staff and rewrite it on four separate staves, using the appropriate clefs for each voice.
  • Mute the bass and compose a new bass line beneath the soprano.
  • Keep the outer voices and invent a new alto or tenor line.
  • Keep the bass and compose an alternative soprano melody.
  • Try a version using mostly stepwise movement, then another with larger leaps. Listen to how the character changes.

These are preliminary experiments in notation, arrangement and composition. There is no need to follow a formal set of harmony rules yet. Focus on listening, exploring the independence of each voice and discovering how different melodic lines work together.
