Rhythm Fundamentals: Feeling Five and Seven Beats

Part 5 · Introducing 5/4 and 7/4 time · Years 7 and above

You've learned to feel four beats in a bar, then three beats. Now let's try two more possibilities: five beats and seven beats. We aren't learning a new way to count notes. We're learning to feel a different length of musical phrase.

Every time signature has a feel

Much of the popular music and dance music we hear is in 4/4. Its four-beat pattern can feel steady, square and familiar. In 3/4, the repeating three-beat pattern often has the gentle turning or swaying feel of a waltz.

But music doesn't have to group its beats in threes or fours. 5/4 has five quarter-note beats in each bar, and 7/4 has seven. Both can feel a little unusual at first, simply because we're less accustomed to hearing them.

Listen for the shape of the bar

4/4: ONE · two · three · four | ONE · two · three · four

3/4: ONE · two · three | ONE · two · three

5/4: ONE · two · three · four · five | ONE · two · three · four · five

7/4: ONE · two · three · four · five · six · seven | ONE · two · three · four · five · six · seven

You might hear five beats as 3 + 2 or 2 + 3, and seven as 4 + 3 or 3 + 4. These are possible ways to feel the larger group, not extra rules you have to memorise.

For a famous example of 5/4, listen to Take Five, made famous by the Dave Brubeck Quartet. Notice how the music moves naturally even though each bar has five beats.

Same exercise, new feeling

Keep the approach from Parts 3 and 4: tap your foot steadily on each beat, and say “ta” whenever a new note begins. Don't worry about calling out the numbers. The goal is to get comfortable with the way the bar comes around.

Start with quarter notes and eighth notes at a comfortable tempo. You can add longer and shorter notes later. Each player makes new rhythms endlessly, so there's no need to get everything right the first time.

Endless rhythm practice in 5/4

Tap 5 even beats with your foot. Say “ta” for each new note. Let the rhythm repeat until the 5-beat grouping starts to feel familiar.

Give yourself time to feel it

At first, five and seven beats might feel like four beats with something extra added. That's fine. Keep your foot steady, listen for the start of each new bar, and allow the pattern to become familiar. The aim isn't speed or perfect counting — it's feeling the pulse.
