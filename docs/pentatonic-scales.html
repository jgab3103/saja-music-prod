Pentatonic Scale Shapes

Modern Guitar · Standard tuning (E–A–D–G–B–E)

The pentatonic scale uses five notes. Explore its major and minor forms across the guitar neck, then learn five movable, two-notes-per-string shapes. Choose a key to transpose every diagram.

Across the fretboard

Every dot belongs to the chosen scale. The outlined dots are the tonic, which gives the scale its name.

Five movable shapes

These patterns cover the same five notes in different regions of the neck. Some positions wrap into the lower octave so all five remain accessible near the nut.

How to practise

  1. Start with Shape 1. Play from the lowest string to the highest, then back down, one note at a time.
  2. Use alternate picking (down–up) and a steady, slow pulse. Keep each note clear.
  3. Learn each shape without looking at the diagram, then connect neighbouring shapes.
  4. Find and pause on the outlined root notes. Listen to how the other notes sound against them.
  5. Improvise a two-bar phrase using just three or four notes. Leave space between ideas.
