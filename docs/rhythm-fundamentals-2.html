Rhythm Fundamentals: Dividing the Beat

Part 2 · Eighth notes, sixteenth notes and subdivisions · Years 7 and above

In Part 1, we learned to feel a steady pulse, count to four and clap rhythms using whole notes, half notes and quarter notes. Before we add anything new, let’s remember what those notes mean.

Before we begin: a quick recap

Imagine a steady pulse: 1, 2, 3, 4. Keep saying those four numbers at the same speed. The pulse continues even when a note lasts longer than one beat.

Three note values we already know

Quarter notes — one beat each

Clap on 1, 2, 3 and 4. Each quarter note begins on a new beat.

Half notes — two beats each

Clap on 1 and 3, but keep counting all four beats. Each half note lasts for two beats.

Whole note — four beats

Clap on 1 and continue counting 2, 3, 4. The whole note lasts for the entire bar.

Now let's divide the beat

So far, our shortest note has lasted one full beat. But we can fit more than one sound inside a beat without making the pulse faster. First we will split each beat into two equal parts (eighth notes). Then we will split it into four equal parts (sixteenth notes).

When we count subdivisions aloud, the numbers still mark the four main beats. We simply add syllables between the numbers to show where the shorter notes belong. Try saying the syllables slowly and evenly before clapping.

1. Two sounds in one beat: eighth notes

Start by saying just 1, 2, 3, 4. Now place an “and” exactly halfway between each pair of numbers: 1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and. Keep the numbers at the same speed as before. You are counting two equal parts of each beat, not speeding up the pulse.

Two eighth notes fit into one quarter-note beat. Say “1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and” evenly. The numbers fall on the beats; each “and” falls halfway between them.

Listen and count

Say the numbers and “ands” aloud. Clap on every eighth note.

2. Four sounds in one beat: sixteenth notes

Once the eighth-note counting feels comfortable, divide each beat in half again. Say “1 e and a” evenly across beat 1, then repeat for beats 2, 3 and 4. The syllables “e” and “a” fit between the number and the “and”, and between the “and” and the next number.

Four sixteenth notes fit into one quarter-note beat. Count “1 e and a, 2 e and a, 3 e and a, 4 e and a” at an even speed. The beat numbers still land at the same places.

Listen and count

Start slowly. Say each syllable clearly before increasing the tempo.

3. The beat stays the same

Compare these three rhythms. The four-beat bar takes the same amount of time in every example. Only the number of notes changes.

A · Quarter notes — one note per beat
B · Eighth notes — two notes per beat
C · Sixteenth notes — four notes per beat

4. Mix the subdivisions

Now try rhythms that combine different note lengths. Keep counting 1 e and a, 2 e and a, 3 e and a, 4 e and a, even when you don't clap on every syllable.

D · Quarter notes and eighth notes
E · Eighth notes and sixteenth notes
F · Quarter, eighth and sixteenth notes

5. Endless subdivision practice

Say the counting aloud and clap only when a new note begins. Choose which note values you want to practise. Each bar has four beats; while one row plays, the other shows the next rhythm. The finished row changes to a new random pattern, so you can keep practising without stopping.

Choose your note values

For an easier challenge, start with quarter and eighth notes. Add sixteenth notes when you're ready. Change the selection while playing to affect newly generated bars.
