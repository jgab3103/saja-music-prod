Composition Study: Bach Chorales · Part 1

The Bach Chorale Explorer

Listening, reading and investigating four-part writing

A chorale is a hymn melody associated with the Lutheran tradition. Johann Sebastian Bach harmonised many such melodies for four voices. Each voice has its own melodic shape, but together they create an evolving harmonic texture.

In this study, we will learn to compose by investigating how music is made. Rather than starting with a list of rules, we will listen to Bach, take his music apart and discover how the individual parts work together.

What we are investigating

How can four independent melodies combine to create a coherent piece of music? By the end of this lesson, try to hear each of the four voices independently and describe the difference between a melody and a harmony.

1. Meet the four voices

Four-part writing is often described as SATB: soprano, alto, tenor and bass. In a chorale, the soprano often carries the hymn melody, the bass shapes the harmonic foundation, and the alto and tenor connect the outer voices. All four are melodic lines in their own right.

3. From melody to harmony

Music moves horizontally through time, but at any instant several notes may be sounding together. Move the slider to freeze an instant in the score. You can inspect the four notes sounding at that moment and hear them as a single sonority.

A sounding note may have started earlier and still be held. Not every group of notes necessarily forms a simple chord.

Exercise 2

Which voice moves?

Compare two successive moments. Which voice or voices change pitch? Select your answer, then check it. Repeated notes at the same pitch do not count as pitch movement.

4. Think like a composer

Choose a short passage and listen to the soprano alone. Then add the bass. The same melody now has a different context. What do the inner voices contribute?

Composition experiment

Use manuscript paper or a piano to invent a different bass line for two measures of the soprano. Try a line that moves mostly by step, then one that uses larger leaps. Sing or play the soprano over each version.
