Rhythm Fundamentals: Finding the Beat

Part 1 · Pulse, counting and note values · Years 7 and above

Rhythm begins with a steady pulse. In this lesson, you'll count four beats and learn how quarter notes, half notes and whole notes fit into a bar. Listen to each example, then try clapping it yourself.

1. Feel the pulse

A pulse is a regular series of beats. Try tapping once on each beat, keeping the time even.

Start at 60 BPM and aim for an even pulse.

2. Count to four

Many pieces of music group their beats into sets of four. Count aloud: 1, 2, 3, 4 — then begin again at 1. Each group is called a bar.

Try counting while the pulse plays. Can you say each number at exactly the same time as the sound?

3. Quarter notes: one beat each

A quarter note lasts for one beat. Clap once on each number.

1234

Four quarter notes fill our four-beat bar.

4. Half notes: two beats each

A half note lasts for two beats. Clap on 1, then let the sound continue in your imagination through 2. Clap again on 3, and let it continue through 4.

1234

Keep counting evenly, even when you aren't clapping.

5. Whole notes: four beats

A whole note lasts for four beats. Clap on 1 and imagine the sound continuing as you count 2, 3, 4.

1234

One note fills the entire bar!

6. Your first rhythm exercises

Say “1, 2, 3, 4” out loud at a steady speed while you play each exercise. Clap only when a new note begins, but keep saying every number, including the beats held by half notes and whole notes. Try it first with the playback, then on your own.

7. Endless rhythm practice

Count 1, 2, 3, 4 aloud, and clap whenever a note begins. Keep counting steadily through half notes and whole notes. Each row is one bar of four beats. As you finish one row, move straight to the next. The completed row changes to a new random rhythm, so you can keep practising as long as you like.
