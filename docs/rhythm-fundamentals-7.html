Rhythm Fundamentals · Part 7

Reading Rhythm Notation

We have been feeling the beat, dividing it into different numbers of notes, and speaking rhythms using ta. Now let's see how musicians write those rhythms down.

How notes fit inside one beat

In 4/4, one quarter note lasts one beat. We can divide that beat into two eighth notes (joined by one beam) or four sixteenth notes (joined by two beams). For other divisions, a small number tells us how many notes share the beat.

Each example fills exactly one quarter-note beat. The notes sit on a treble staff, so you can see their stems, beams and tuplet numbers clearly.

Endless exercise — read, feel and say

Keep a steady four-beat pulse with your foot. Say “ta” whenever a note begins. Follow the notation as it plays. The beat stays steady even when the notes within it become faster or slower.

Notes in each beat (choose at least one)

Two bars of 4/4, one per line. As one row plays, the other refreshes with a new rhythm.

Each bar has four quarter-note beats. The exercise uses one staff, with pitches kept between C4 and A5. Sound is a simple click so you can concentrate on rhythm.
