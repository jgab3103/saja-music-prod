Waltz Excercise

A simple Chopin-inspired waltz pattern. The left hand never changes: C3 on beat 1, then a closed-position C minor chord in second inversion (G–C–E♭) on beats 2 and 3. In the right hand, place your thumb on C, F or G and play one quarter note followed by four eighth notes. Repeat, change just a few notes and begin making your own melodies. Both hands have been raised one octave; the entire exercise transposes with the key selector. These are original exercises, not excerpts from Chopin.

How to practise

    Pedal: If using the sustain pedal, lift and reapply it at the start of each new left-hand bass–chord–chord figure (each bar). Avoid sustaining across the barline.

    The waltz feel

    Count ONE–two–three: the tonic an octave above the original bass register on beat 1, then the same second-inversion tonic-minor chord on beats 2 and 3. Keep the accompaniment exactly the same in every bar.

    First learn the left hand alone. When it flows without conscious effort, let the right hand sing over it.

    The right-hand rhythm is always quarter, eighth, eighth, eighth, eighth (1 + ½ + ½ + ½ + ½ = 3 beats). Finger 1 plays the first note of every bar. Each group of five notes stays in one comfortable hand position.
