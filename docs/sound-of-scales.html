About the included scales

The Sound of Scales Lab is intended to be an exploratory listening resource, rather than an authoritative reference on scales, modes or musical traditions.

There is extensive and fascinating scholarship on the naming, spelling, classification, tuning and temperament of scales across different historical periods and musical cultures. Even the concept of a scale, and how it is understood and used, varies considerably between traditions.

In this lab, we have represented a wide variety of pitch collections using twelve-tone equal temperament, with C as a common reference point. Our aim is to provide different contexts for listening to different groupings of the twelve chromatic tones, exploring how their relationships create different musical colours and experiences.

This distinction is particularly important for the Indian classical pitch collections included here. A raga is much more than a collection of notes. Its identity can involve characteristic melodic phrases, ascending and descending patterns, ornamentation, tuning and conventions of performance. The simplified pitch collections presented here do not attempt to create music in these traditions, but does seek to introduce listeners to some of the amazing characteristics that can be found in them.

We encourage listeners to treat the scale names as starting points for exploration, rather than definitive classifications, and to investigate the rich musical histories and traditions associated with them.

A small listening experiment

Begin with only stepwise movement. Then raise the thirds and listen for a more arpeggiated shape. Add fourths to hear how wider leaps change the atmosphere. Try muting different scale notes while the C drone holds steady. Finally, change modes while the C drone continues: the root stays fixed, but the sense of colour shifts around it.

There is no score to follow and no correct answer. This is an exercise in hearing the character of a scale through repeated, evolving musical gestures.
